# Nuclear Atlas: stakeholder map

Research date: September 4, 2026. Scope: civilian nuclear energy, fuel cycle, waste, and decommissioning. This replaces the six activity buckets as the basis for persona design. It does not cover the full stakeholder universe for nuclear medicine, weapons, or industrial isotope applications.

## Method and evidence

Start with explicit stakeholder identification, not topics in a dashboard. The IAEA's 2021 NG-G-5.1 guide, section 2.1 and Figure 1 (printed pages 5–6), identifies communities near actual or potential sites, government, regulators, owners/operators, suppliers, employees, unions, media, NGOs, academia, and neighbouring countries. Its list is explicitly non-exhaustive. Section 4 differentiates the responsibilities of government, regulator, and owner/operator.

## Role-based archetypes

These labels translate documented stakeholder groups into people a product can address. They are a synthesis, not an official universal list or a ranking of Atlas customers.

| Person / role | Relationship | Evidence basis |
|---|---|---|
| Community member | Lives near a proposed or existing facility; may be a tenant, homeowner, nearby landowner, parent or local business owner | IAEA guide; NRC public involvement |
| Tribal government representative | Represents a sovereign government with potentially affected interests; not interchangeable with a generic public commenter | NRC Tribal Policy Statement; NEA |
| Local or regional official | Represents a jurisdiction hosting or affected by development and closure | IAEA guide; NEA |
| Emergency planner or public-health official | Holds preparedness or public-protection responsibilities | FEMA REP |
| Nuclear worker or union representative | Works at the facility or represents the workforce | IAEA Figure 1 |
| Project developer | Advances a proposed facility through siting and development | Translation of IAEA owner/project organization and NRC applicant roles |
| Facility owner or operator | Owns or runs an operating reactor or fuel-cycle facility | IAEA section 4 |
| Supplier or contractor | Provides fuel, equipment, engineering, construction or services | IAEA Figure 1 |
| Fuel buyer / procurement manager | Purchases fuel and related services for a developer or operator | Product-specific specialization of owner/operator role; DOE HALEU context |
| Electricity buyer / utility resource planner | Evaluates or contracts for electricity supply | Product-specific specialization of business/industry; DOE commercialization context |
| Investor or lender | Supplies capital and evaluates exposure | IAEA May 2025 bulletin |
| Insurer | Evaluates insurance exposure; distinct from equity investment | IAEA May 2025 bulletin |
| Regulator or licensing reviewer | Independently reviews compliance and applications | IAEA section 4; NRC |
| Policymaker / government programme official | Shapes energy policy and public programmes | IAEA section 4 |
| Waste-management or decommissioning professional | Manages storage, disposal, closure or remediation | IAEA 2025 conference; guide lifecycle scope |
| Researcher / technical expert | Produces or evaluates technical evidence | IAEA Figure 1; conference |
| Journalist | Investigates and communicates developments | IAEA Figure 1 |
| Civil-society organization representative | Organizes around environmental, economic or other public interests | IAEA Figure 1; NEA |

Cross-cutting constituencies include ratepayers, neighbouring jurisdictions/countries, future generations, and people with barriers to participation. Future generations cannot be treated as an interviewable current user. Indigenous rights-holder categories require jurisdiction-specific treatment. People may occupy several roles. Support for or opposition to nuclear is not itself a persona.

## Worked archetype: community member near a proposed reactor

Context: lives near a proposed site and needs to understand the proposal, possible local effects, responsible authorities, and opportunities to participate. Do not assume support, opposition, technical knowledge, or that concerns are merely an information deficit.

Candidate questions, written for Atlas rather than quoted from research:
1. What is proposed, exactly where, and who is responsible?
2. Is it an announcement, an application, an approved project, or an active construction site?
3. What does the environmental review say about local water, land use and construction effects?
4. Where can I find official safety and emergency-planning information applicable to this project?
5. What spent fuel or waste would remain at the site, and what is the documented plan?
6. What local benefits and burdens are claimed, and what evidence supports them?
7. When can I attend a meeting, submit a comment, or request information?
8. Which government or organization has responsibility for the issue I care about?

NRC participation guidance supports the permitting, environmental-review, and participation topics. FEMA supports preparedness responsibilities. DOE's RFI captures actual concerns about waste siting, property and transport impacts, trust and consent; those findings must not be presented as interviews with residents near new reactors. Exact wording and priority of the eight questions remain unvalidated.

## Implications for the tab and future controls

Use person labels (Community member, Fuel buyer, Project developer), not activity labels (Local understanding, Supply chain, Project development). Separate people with different authority and decisions, especially residents, local government, and Tribal governments. Do not combine reporters and market analysts simply because both read documents.

Maintain a broad stakeholder map, then choose a smaller set of intended product users separately. Neither stakeholder status nor an official agency publication establishes demand for Atlas. The previous persona-dashboard-roadmap.md is superseded as a stakeholder taxonomy; its question bank is provisional and must be reassessed against these roles.

Before changing dashboard controls: each question needs a role, lifecycle context, evidence source, required fields, and an honest answerability assessment. Public questions about a proposal should not be rewritten as questions about which records happen to exist in the current Atlas dataset.
